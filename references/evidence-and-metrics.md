# Evidence, attribution, metrics, and NDA

## Evidence ladder

Use the strongest available evidence and do not upgrade its certainty:

1. **Achieved:** verified post-launch or measured result personally attributable to the candidate.
2. **Contributed:** measured team/product result where the candidate owned a material part of the work.
3. **Team outcome:** relevant result, but personal attribution is limited; use team language.
4. **Forecast/potential:** modeled or expected future value; label it explicitly.
5. **NDA-sensitive:** real evidence that cannot be disclosed; generalize or omit.

## Safe transformations

```text
Achieved: Increased conversion by 18% by redesigning the onboarding flow.
Contributed: Contributed to an 18% increase in conversion by redesigning onboarding and partnering with PM and engineering.
Team outcome: The team increased conversion by 18%; owned onboarding redesign and usability validation.
Forecast: Identified a potential $XM opportunity by 2030 through pricing and upsell concepts.
NDA-safe: Improved monetization and validated four pricing hypotheses; commercial figures available in interview.
```

Do not turn `potential`, `planned`, `target`, `forecast`, `team`, `helped`, or `contributed` into an unqualified personal achievement. Do not expose internal product names, revenue, customer counts, or experiment details unless the user confirms they are public or NDA-safe.

## Minimum metric questions

Ask only what changes the wording: metric name, baseline/control, comparison period, result, candidate's exact contribution, whether the result was shipped or forecast, and whether the number is public. If the user cannot answer, use a qualitative outcome or a safe range rather than inventing precision.

## Work authorization

Do not state `authorized to work`, `no sponsorship required`, or `eligible for 1099` without verified evidence. A neutral location line may say `Seeking remote freelance and contract opportunities with international teams`; application forms must be answered truthfully. Treat US employment, contractor arrangements, foreign employers, immigration status, and tax residency as separate questions and recommend qualified advice when material.
